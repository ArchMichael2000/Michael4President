America Reborn

America Reborn gives civic expression to a program of rebuilding homes, productive life, and the institutions a country must be able to maintain.

America Reborn is the civic expression of the political work gathered in this book. It speaks to a country whose people should be able to build a life, maintain their common institutions, and leave their children an inheritance they can use.

Generative Sovereignty supplies the philosophical foundation. Through it I developed Makerism and Hearthism and drew together older traditions of government and economic life. America Reborn gives that work a public language. The phrase should call to mind things a citizen can recognize in his own town and household.

The civic purpose and the programs

The program names describe different fields of work. Select one to see how it connects national renewal to measures in the policybook.

Work that leads to a livelihood and a stake

A Makers Economy concerns the conditions in which useful work can support a settled life. Wages, practical training, productive investment, and access to ownership affect whether a person can build a future in the place where he works.

The Henry Ford Act gathers economic and industrial proposals. Apprenticeship and the preservation of living trades attend to the knowledge that factories and workshops need. Their civic purpose can be judged through the opportunities available to the person expected to carry the work.

Producerism

The country as a responsibility to be carried

The New Home Front names the broader program of national reconstruction. A home depends on more than its walls: work, care, dependable infrastructure, and lawful institutions affect what the household can sustain. The country has to attend to those connections.

The New Homestead Act and the Family Formation Act address different points in that account. Kinship measures concern the proximity of generations. These proposals should leave people better able to perform responsibilities that government itself cannot assume in their place.

Hearthism

Productive life must have somewhere to continue

Bring It Home expresses the recovery of productive life within the country. A lost enterprise can take a training route and much of a town's work with it. Restoring the ability to produce involves people and places as well as a financial decision.

The civic promise needs an account of where the work will take place and what it will require. Domestic industrial capacity and local knowledge connect national independence to a livelihood citizens can recognize. A program should explain how another generation can enter that work.

Collegium

What we are trying to restore

A young person should have a credible path from education into useful work and a home. A family should be able to plan beyond the next rent increase. An older person should remain part of a community that knows him. These ordinary expectations depend on conditions that policy can strengthen or allow to deteriorate.

A country also needs reliable power, functioning transport, and the ability to produce what its security requires. The affection people feel for their home cannot keep a bridge in service or replace a lost trade. Those tasks require money, skill, and institutions willing to carry their responsibilities for years.

Restoration addresses this work. It repairs what has deteriorated and makes room to build what is needed. A new method of construction can help a family establish a home. New engineering can restore the usefulness of an old system. The question is what the change enables people to preserve and do.

The country as people encounter it

National life takes a local form. People experience it in the roads they travel, the buildings they use, the schools their children attend, and the work available nearby. The history of a place lives partly in these daily arrangements. A town can retain its name while losing most of what once allowed its people to remain there.

Regional heritage and public architecture belong within this account. A building should serve its occupants and remain worth maintaining. Honest materials and workmanship leave the next generation with something usable. Public spaces should give people a reason to meet and a place in which their shared life can be seen.

Local differences can contribute to a common identity. A farming community and an industrial city carry different knowledge and obligations. Both need standing in decisions whose consequences they will bear. National coordination must be capable of hearing those conditions rather than treating every place as interchangeable.

Memory and public conduct

We can honor an achievement and give an honest account of a failure. Gratitude gives us reasons to preserve what we have received. Responsibility requires us to repair what has gone wrong. A truthful public memory makes both duties intelligible.

Monuments and ceremonies help a people remember. Their meaning depends on conduct beyond the occasion. A government honoring service must answer for the treatment of those who served. A country praising craftsmanship must keep open the routes through which another generation can learn the work. The ordinary policy and the public statement should belong to the same account.

The citizen carries duties here as well. A national office cannot take a parent's place or perform every obligation between neighbors. Public institutions should support the conditions in which people can carry these responsibilities themselves. A common life becomes fragile when everyone expects someone else to maintain it.

The names of the work

The program names in the policybook describe different fields of action. A Makers Economy concerns productive work and ownership. The New Home Front gathers the broader work of national reconstruction. Bring It Home expresses the effort to recover productive life and its place in the country.

The acts turn these purposes into proposals that can be examined. The New Homestead Act addresses the ground on which a citizen can establish a life. The Henry Ford Act gathers industrial and economic measures. Family Formation and Kinship Preservation concern the conditions in which households can form and generations can remain connected.

America Reborn is the civic idiom across this work. It does not replace the names of the philosophies or the particulars of an act. A useful public language allows citizens to understand what the program is trying to accomplish and then examine whether its mechanisms can accomplish it.

What a citizen should be able to judge

A claim of renewal should lead to a concrete account. What can be built? Who will maintain it? Can the person doing the work afford a settled life? Does the institution have a means of correction when it fails? These questions connect a national purpose to what happens after an announcement.

The next generation will receive the condition of the country we leave. Its inheritance includes obligations as well as assets, habits as well as buildings. America Reborn names the work of making that inheritance sound enough for people to live in and take responsibility for themselves.

Questions and answers

Is America Reborn a philosophy or a program name?

It is the civic idiom for the work assembled through Generative Sovereignty. Makerism and Hearthism are philosophies developed from that foundation. A Makers Economy, The New Home Front, and Bring It Home name programs or fields of action within the policybook.

How can restoration include new technology?

A responsibility to preserve a useful inheritance can require new methods. Better engineering can restore a system's usefulness, and a new construction method can help a family establish a home. The purpose is to leave people able to live and take responsibility for what they have received.

What should a promise of national renewal explain?

It should identify what will change in ordinary life and who will carry the work. A maintained road, an attainable home, or a training route gives a citizen something to examine. The policybook's acts and mechanisms can then be judged by whether they produce that result.

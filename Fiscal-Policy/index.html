A household should be able to understand what it pays, what it owns, and what its contributions provide. This fiscal program combines a liquid-income tax with dedicated retirement and healthcare funding, corporate taxes, and other assigned revenue. Each payment has a defined base and destination. Household-owned money remains the household's property when the national figures are added together.

The proposals connect public funding to the conditions in which people can build a secure life. Essential services need dependable resources. Enterprise needs rules under which investment and useful work remain worthwhile. The rates and projections here follow Innovative Tax Plan Overhaul V27 and Deep Tax Burden Study V35.

Keep the purpose attached to the payment

A tax bill affects the money a family can use today. A retirement contribution supports an obligation continuing into the future. An HSA contribution enters an account the household owns. Understanding those differences allows someone to judge both the immediate charge and what it provides.

The same discipline applies nationally. General Treasury revenue finances its assigned operations. Retirement and healthcare funds carry obligations of their own. A protected fund does not become available for ordinary spending simply because its balance appears in a consolidated total. Public finance becomes easier to follow when each institution answers for the money entrusted to it.

The system also needs to remain capable of delivery. Paying for healthcare requires care capacity. Authorizing construction requires workers, materials, and maintenance. The financial mechanisms should support those responsibilities without concealing their costs or confusing startup investment with recurring operations.

One liquid income definition

The model uses a $20.471 trillion liquid personal-income base. It includes covered cash income available to the individual: wages, previously untaxed retirement distributions, royalties, taxable settlements, and foreign cash income. Social Security benefits, public assistance, and the return of after-tax principal receive their specified exclusions.

The standard individual income-tax rate is ten percent. Citizens contribute three percent to Social Security. Citizen HSA tiers are two, three, and five percent, with corresponding federal supplements of three, two, and zero percent. These supplements provide the specified citizen-side funding floor.

The full-time-student wage exemption and qualifying youth and elderly rules retain their stated treatment. V35 applies ten percent to its active aggregate liquid-income base; an individual's statutory classification determines his treatment. Using a common definition keeps the covered income consistent across personal tax and dedicated contributions.

The Well Being Tax and dedicated contributions

The Well Being Tax names the personal and compensation-based contributions supporting Social Security and healthcare. Those receipts remain in their assigned funds. The National Continuity Contribution, or NCC, uses a separate company-value base.

Employers contribute three percent of compensation transferred to citizen employees for Social Security. Covered compensation includes cash wages, bonuses, stock, and other equity awards at their specified fair value. State and local governments contribute as employers on the same terms, with their compensation recorded once in the national base.

Under the baseline citizenship system, non-citizens use a Resident Contributor Number and contribute seven percent, alongside a separate seven percent employer contribution. The receipts support the Social Security trust under Citizen-Only Social Security. The Two-Tier Citizenship proposal in Hyper-Reform has its own terms if enacted.

The replacement system abolishes the legacy payroll tax and establishes these specified contributions. An employer's obligation follows compensation. NCC follows enterprise value. Keeping their bases distinct shows how each charge affects employment, compensation, and the enterprise itself.

Household effects

V35 compares 2024 single-filer federal income and employee payroll taxes with the proposed personal tax, citizen Social Security contribution, and assigned HSA tier. The calculator applies those proposed individual rates to the covered liquid income entered.

In the $80,000 wage example, the compared charges fall from $15,561 to $12,800, a reduction of $2,761. At $70,000, they fall from $12,596 to $11,200, a reduction of $1,396. The retiree example receives $60,000 in pension income and $30,000 in excluded Social Security benefits; the comparison moves from $10,222 to $9,000.

These comparisons cover federal individual charges. Employer contributions, state taxes, credits, itemized deductions, and current insurance premiums are outside their scope. The HSA portion remains household-owned money with its healthcare purpose. Source: V35, Household_Incidence, A2:N10.

Corporate income and productive deployment

The corporate statutory rate is fifteen percent. V35 uses a ten percent effective Treasury rate after the active reinvestment, bonus, and productive-deployment provisions. A qualifying reduction must correspond to business activity or employee benefit established in law. The statutory rate, reductions, and minimum obligation together determine what the company owes.

Estate and trust distributions receive separate three percent treatment. Covered entity-level deferral receives ten percent source-level treatment, with credits coordinating later distributions of the same economic income. Those credits record tax already paid when income reaches a later tax event.

Other mechanisms include the Outsourcing Penalty, Fair Profit Tax, Loan Scumming provisions, Protectorate Fee, resource royalties, energy-export excise, Idle Wealth Levy, Offshore Capital Export Duty, and high-wealth collection measures. Explicit adjustments remove overlapping collection so the same receipt is counted once.

The Outsourcing Penalty applies the ten percent rule to qualifying companies outsourcing more than half their manufacturing. The Protectorate Fee uses one percent of the covered expenditure base of seventy-seven countries. Fair Profit Tax follows the policybook's product-cost multiple mechanism. Each has a separate calculation base in the workbook.

Social Security and National Continuity

NCC assesses covered enterprise value at 0.73 percent for companies above the one-million-dollar threshold. V35's central Year 0 covered base is $68 trillion, producing $496.4 billion. The model uses a 1.2 percent annual growth assumption for contribution and valuation bases.

The receipts first close Social Security's remaining gap. The residual goes to the National Inheritance and Reserve Fund, or NIRF. At Year 10, the model projects $559.290 billion in NCC receipts, divided into $181.224 billion for Social Security and $378.065 billion for NIRF.

Social Security also receives dedicated contributions, investment income, and assigned lending earnings. Healthcare remains separately funded. Routine NHDC support for Social Security is zero in the active National Continuity structure.

V27 separately specifies treatment for qualified family farms and closely held manufacturing: a five-million-dollar allowance, a quarter-percent band through ten million dollars, and the full rate above it. V35 retains its central headline calculation and a separate carve-out sensitivity schedule. These are distinct cases, and their figures should retain that identification.

Healthcare and public capacity

Healthcare funding includes citizen HSAs, federal HSA supplements, employer and public sponsor funding, assigned sin-tax receipts, and external NHDC earnings. Adding them together does not change their ownership or assigned purposes.

V35 starts with $5.300 trillion in annual national health expenditure. Its twenty percent structural-savings case produces a $4.240 trillion cost basis. Domestic provider margins remain within the system. External funding is counted separately, and international licensing enters the consolidated healthcare balance.

The NHDC waterfall maintains a $120 billion healthcare reserve and provides for approved research and productive expansion. The remaining distributable amount is divided equally between NIRF and the Citizen Wealth Fund. Distribution follows those preceding obligations.

Land ownership and the land account

The land-value tax falls on land, with primary-family-home and qualifying small-business protections maintained. Improvements are distinct from the land beneath them. The model carries a $2.233 trillion annual land-revenue target.

That target equals 2.592 percent of the $86.145 trillion real-estate envelope used in the model. The central sensitivity treats forty percent of the envelope as land and fifty percent of that land as taxable. This produces a $17.229 trillion base and a 12.960 percent implied annual rate.

The distinction between the whole real-estate envelope and taxable land matters to the rate. Applying exemptions, separating structures, and identifying the receiving government are necessary parts of the proposed charge. Property-tax replacement also needs to specify which services each level of government continues to finance.

Fiscal stewardship over time

The operating projection covers recurring replacement operations. Startup capitalization, construction, and the delivery schedule belong in a transition appropriation. Separating them shows what the system needs to begin and what it expects to sustain after it is operating.

Borrowing to acquire an asset creates a liability alongside the asset. Investment returns become available under the fund's withdrawal rules. Protected balances retain their purposes in consolidated national figures. These distinctions let citizens judge a proposal without treating every financial asset as money available to spend now.

A funded project must also be deliverable. Skilled labor, materials, energy, and maintenance have to be available when the work requires them. Institutions should answer for both the expenditure and the service it produces. The household at the end of that chain deserves a working service and a clear explanation of its share of the cost.

Questions and answers

How much of the surplus is available for ordinary government spending?

The model records a separate General Government operating balance of $318.956 billion. The larger consolidated surplus also includes healthcare funds, money assigned to the National Inheritance and Reserve Fund, and protected-capital receipts. Those amounts keep their assigned purposes. Adding them together does not make the whole total available for ordinary Treasury spending.

How is the Well Being Tax different from NCC?

They charge different things and direct the money to different uses. The Well Being Tax is based on personal income and compensation, with payments assigned to Social Security and healthcare. The National Continuity Contribution (NCC) is based on covered company value. It fills Social Security's remaining funding gap, then sends the rest to the National Inheritance and Reserve Fund.

Who owns the money in an HSA?

The household owns it. Citizen contributions and federal supplements help fund healthcare under the system's rules, while other healthcare receipts retain their assigned purposes. Combining the national figures does not transfer the household's HSA money to the Treasury or make it available for unrelated government spending.

Where are startup and construction costs included?

They belong in the transition appropriation: the funding needed to capitalize the system, build capacity, and bring it into service. The operating projection shows recurring revenue and costs at each stage. If the government borrows to buy an asset, it must also record the debt. Owning the asset does not erase the obligation to repay it.

Where do these rates and figures come from?

Innovative Tax Plan Overhaul V27 sets out the rates and design. Deep Tax Burden Study V35 supplies the calculations for revenue, operating stages, and household examples. Its main projection is separate from the calculation showing how special treatment for qualified family farms and closely held manufacturing changes the results.

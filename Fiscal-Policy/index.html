Public finance must sustain the commonwealth without consuming the conditions of its future. Taxes must have an intelligible base. Public obligations must have an identified source of payment. Protected funds must remain protected. The national account must show what is collected, what is owed, what is transferred, and what remains.

The fiscal architecture joins General Government, Social Security, Healthcare, and the public funds within one reconciled account. The current tax baseline is Innovative Tax Plan Overhaul V27 and Deep Tax Burden Study V35, the companion tax architecture and fiscal model.

One liquid income definition

The model uses a common liquid personal-income base of $20.471 trillion. It includes income actually available to the individual, including wages, previously untaxed retirement distributions, royalties, taxable settlements, foreign cash income, and other covered cash receipts. Social Security benefits, public assistance, and the return of after-tax principal receive their specified exclusions.

The individual income-tax rate is ten percent. The citizen Social Security contribution is three percent. Citizen HSA payments follow the two, three, and five percent tiers, with federal supplements maintaining the specified citizen-side funding floor.

The full-time-student wage exemption and the qualifying youth and elderly treatment remain policy rules. The aggregate model applies ten percent to its active liquid-income base; household treatment follows the statutory classification.

This common definition gives the household one intelligible account of the income entering the system. It also prevents different ledgers from quietly describing the same income in incompatible ways.

The Well Being Tax and dedicated contributions

The Well Being Tax is the policy book's common name for the personal and compensation-based contributions assigned to Social Security and healthcare. Its accounts remain separate. The National Continuity Contribution is a distinct assessment on company value.

Employers contribute three percent of compensation transferred to citizen employees for Social Security. Cash wages, bonuses, stock, and other covered equity compensation enter that base at their specified fair value. State and local governments contribute as employers on the same terms, with their compensation shown once in the national account.

Under the book's baseline citizenship system, non-citizens contribute through a Resident Contributor Number at seven percent, with a separate seven percent employer contribution. These receipts support the Social Security trust under the eligibility rules in Citizen-Only Social Security. The proposed Two-Tier Citizenship instrument sits in Hyper-Reform and supplies its own terms if enacted.

Abolition of the legacy payroll tax therefore accompanies replacement contributions with stated bases and destinations. The employer contribution remains an obligation on compensation; the NCC follows enterprise value. Hiring and company valuation must not be treated as interchangeable tax bases.

Corporate income and productive deployment

The corporate statutory rate is fifteen percent. V35 uses a ten percent effective Treasury rate after the active reinvestment, bonus, and productive-deployment provisions.

Productive deployment must be identifiable. A claimed reinvestment should correspond to the business activity, employee benefit, or other qualifying purpose established in law. The corporate rate, the available reductions, and the minimum obligation must be stated together.

Estate and trust distributions carry the separate three percent treatment. Covered entity-level deferral receives ten percent source-level treatment with credits coordinating later distributions of the same economic income. The tax follows the gain while the credit preserves one coherent account of what has already been paid.

The revenue architecture also includes the Outsourcing Penalty, Fair Profit Tax, Loan Scumming mechanisms, Protectorate Fee, resource royalties, energy-export excise, Idle Wealth Levy, Offshore Capital Export Duty, and high-wealth collection measures. The model records overlapping collection channels through explicit adjustments rather than counting the same receipts twice.

The Protectorate Fee model applies one percent to the covered expenditure base of seventy-seven countries. Fair Profit Tax uses the policy book's product-cost multiple mechanism. Their calculation bases remain separately traceable in the companion workbook.

Social Security and National Continuity

The National Continuity Contribution connects retirement funding to the value of productive enterprise. V35 applies the 0.73 percent rate to covered enterprises above the one-million-dollar threshold, using a central covered enterprise-value base of $68 trillion at Year 0, producing $496.4 billion. The contribution and valuation bases grow at the model's 1.2 percent annual assumption.

The annual waterfall first fills the Social Security gap. Remaining NCC receipts flow to the National Inheritance and Reserve Fund. At Year 10, the model projects $559.290 billion in NCC receipts, $181.224 billion allocated to Social Security, and $378.065 billion allocated to NIRF.

Social Security also receives its dedicated contributions, investment income, and assigned lending earnings. Healthcare operates on its own ledger. Routine NHDC transfers to Social Security are zero in the active National Continuity structure.

The V27 design section separately develops qualified family-farm and closely held manufacturing treatment: a five-million-dollar allowance, a quarter-percent intermediate band through ten million dollars, and the full rate above that level. V35 retains its central headline alongside the separate carve-out sensitivity schedule.

Healthcare and public capacity

The healthcare account combines citizen HSAs, the federal HSA supplement, employer funding, public sponsor funding, assigned sin-tax receipts, and external NHDC earnings.

V35's core healthcare calculation begins with $5.300 trillion in annual national health expenditure and applies its twenty percent structural-savings case, producing a $4.240 trillion cost basis. The model keeps domestic provider margins within the system and counts external funding separately. International licensing enters the consolidated healthcare balance.

The NHDC waterfall maintains the $120 billion healthcare reserve, provides for approved research and productive expansion, and divides the remaining distributable amount equally between NIRF and the Citizen Wealth Fund. The purpose is durable care capacity with an intelligible account of its funding.

Land ownership and the land account

The land-value tax applies to land rather than improvements, with the primary-family-home and qualifying small-business protections maintained. The model carries a $2.233 trillion annual land-revenue target.

V35 presents the target beside a real-estate envelope of $86.145 trillion. The target equals 2.592 percent of that envelope. Its central sensitivity assigns forty percent of the envelope to land and fifty percent of that land to the taxable share, producing a $17.229 trillion base and a 12.960 percent implied annual rate.

These are the model's base and rate relationships. The land account must keep land, structures, exemptions, productive use, and territorial revenue assignment distinct. Property-tax replacement and additional federal receipts must be reconciled with the services financed at each level of government.

Household effects

The workbook's household illustrations compare 2024 single-filer federal income and employee payroll taxes with the proposed liquid-income tax, citizen Social Security contribution, and assigned HSA tier.

At $80,000 in wages, the illustration moves from $15,561 to $12,800, a reduction of $2,761. At $70,000, it moves from $12,596 to $11,200, a reduction of $1,396. The retiree illustration uses $60,000 in pension income and $30,000 in Social Security benefits, moving from $10,222 to $9,000.

The comparison's scope is federal individual charges. Employer contributions, state taxes, credits, itemized deductions, and current insurance premiums are accounted for outside these examples.

Fiscal stewardship over time

The operating projection and the transition appropriation serve different purposes. The former describes recurring replacement operations. The latter finances startup capitalization, construction, delivery schedules, and the movement from existing arrangements into the replacement system. Those costs belong in their own explicit account.

The same discipline governs public investment. Borrowing to acquire an asset creates both an asset and a liability. Investment returns become spendable receipts under the fund's withdrawal rules. A protected balance keeps its purpose when the national accounts are consolidated.

Fiscal management must also answer to real capacity. Money appropriated for a project does not itself supply skilled labor, energy, materials, or maintenance. The financial plan and the delivery plan must meet in the same decision.

The commonwealth should be able to show what it can sustain today, what it is building for tomorrow, and what obligations accompany both. That is the fiscal expression of Generative Sovereignty.

Questions and answers

Is the consolidated surplus all available for ordinary spending?

The consolidated figure joins balances and protected allocations across the architecture. General Government's operating balance is the separate $318.956 billion line. Healthcare's balance, the NCC residual to NIRF, and the protected-capital receipt retain their assigned purposes. Consolidating the accounts makes the total visible while preserving the destinations of its parts.

Why are the Well Being Tax and NCC different mechanisms?

The Well Being Tax follows liquid personal income and compensation, with receipts assigned separately to Social Security and healthcare. NCC follows covered enterprise value. Its waterfall closes the remaining Social Security gap and sends the residual to NIRF. Hiring, compensation, company value, and the ownership of an HSA therefore remain different things in the account.

How do household-owned HSAs appear in the architecture?

Citizen HSA contributions remain household-owned healthcare money. Public supplements and assigned healthcare receipts follow the healthcare ledger. An aggregate health-funding total must preserve the distinction between household funds, public transfers, and external earnings. The healthcare account explains how care is financed without turning every dollar in the system into unrestricted Treasury revenue.

How are transition costs treated?

Recurring operating projections describe the replacement system at the stated stage. Startup capitalization, construction, and the schedules by which capacity enters service belong in the transition appropriation. Borrowing to acquire a public asset creates both an asset and a liability; later earnings become available under the relevant fund's rules.

The purpose of the public balance sheet

American fiscal policy must secure affordable essentials, productive employment, dependable public services, and public obligations the nation can actually pay. Recovery restores supply and household stability. Long-term management finances permanent commitments, builds productive capacity, protects savings, and reduces the debt burden. Inflation never substitutes for revenue, and an investment forecast never substitutes for cash.

The replacement system joins three disciplines: a financial account showing who pays, a capacity account showing what the country can produce, and a public balance sheet showing assets alongside every enforceable liability. A balanced appropriation can still bid up scarce materials. A profitable public asset can still leave Treasury short of money this month. Each decision must pass the test that applies to it.

Institutions and responsibility

Congress authorizes taxes, appropriations, guarantees, borrowing, and program commitments. Treasury manages cash, debt, fiscal facilities, and consolidated accounts. The Office of Management and Budget coordinates implementation and agency delivery. Energy and other production agencies repair the constraints within their jurisdictions. States administer authorized household and work-sharing assistance through existing systems.

The Congressional Budget Office independently scores legislation and alternative paths. The Government Accountability Office verifies accounts, program performance, and realized savings. BEA, BLS, and EIA retain independent statistical methods. Neither an executive coordination group nor a monetary authority instructs these institutions to produce favorable findings.

The existing book replaces the Federal Reserve with the National Monetary Authority. The proposed framework assigns monetary operations, collateralized liquidity, payment continuity, and the associated supervisory responsibilities by statute. The transition transfers lawful assets, liabilities, reserve accounts, operational systems, and outstanding commitments. It cancels no Treasury obligation or bank deposit. The United States creates no Economy Czar.

Crisis diagnosis and activation

The proposed crisis standard requires Treasury, the monetary authority, and responsible production agencies to publish a diagnosis within thirty days of emergency activation. It separates constrained supply, excessive aggregate spending, disrupted market liquidity, and insolvency. The diagnosis states the binding constraint, available remedies, financing, delivery timetable, and expected effects on essential access, employment, and persistent inflation.

A shortage demands additional supply and continuity of useful production. A supply-driven price increase alone does not justify automatically raising borrowing costs across the economy. The monetary authority pauses further broad tightening unless it publishes evidence of persistent broad inflation and explains why restraint produces less total harm than feasible alternatives. A pause does not require a rate cut. Decisions account for expectations, exchange rates, credit spreads, real borrowing costs, and policy lags. Falling expected inflation must not make an unchanged nominal rate silently more restrictive.

The proposed reassessment trigger is a three-month unemployment average at least half a percentage point above its lowest three-month average in the preceding twelve months, or a verified essential-supply disruption accompanied by rising arrears or credit stress. A trigger initiates diagnosis and funded action; it grants no unlimited spending authority.

Household security and essential continuity

The existing $10 billion Small Business Surge Reserve supplies the standing administrative channel. Congress establishes assistance formulas, appropriation limits, release conditions, and repayment or financing schedules before a crisis. Additional emergency appropriations expand the response through that channel; the standing reserve is not the limit of a national emergency response.

Proposed support prioritizes temporary income-tested food and utility assistance, work sharing, verified wage continuity, and essential repairs. Employment payments depend on actual retained workers and hours. An advertised vacancy earns nothing. Aid tapers with demonstrated recovery rather than an arbitrary claim that the emergency has ended.

Temporary continuity facilities support operators viable before the disruption through secured advances, limited guarantees, and purchases of indispensable delivery services. Private lenders retain meaningful exposure. Owners bear losses first, and recipients suspend distributions and executive windfalls while emergency obligations remain outstanding. Insolvent businesses restructure; additional debt does not make them solvent.

The National Lending Authority administers authorized lending without changing its fixed domestic terms. Non-housing credit retains the ten percent true annual rate and existing fees. Housing retains ten percent noncompounding interest on unpaid principal, its stated principal-repayment terms, and separate repayment of accrued interest without further interest. Congress finances any additional subsidy openly through an appropriation or guarantee. Public contracts purchase verified service and never reimburse a fuel bill already paid by another program.

Proposed facilities expire after twenty-four months unless renewed with an identified purpose, financing, and exposure ceiling. Treasury separately publishes disbursements, guarantees, estimated losses, recoveries, and public equity.

Purchasing power and monetary discipline

The proposed purchasing-power reference is zero persistent consumer-price inflation, with an initial normal range of minus one to plus one percent over rolling twelve months. It is an operating reference subject to published evaluation, not a guarantee for every price or month. Productivity-driven price declines belong to households when output, employment, and debt-paying incomes remain healthy. The authority does not manufacture compensating inflation merely to erase earlier beneficial declines.

Broad price declines accompanied by falling labor income, sustained unemployment, rising defaults, or damaged essential supply require reassessment and funded stabilization. The government repairs shortages without forcing the entire price index back to an old number through unemployment.

The existing Resource-Backed Dollar requires audited asset coverage, without metal redemption, fixed parity, or compulsory monetary contraction after an asset-price fall. The proposed implementation law defines the covered monetary liabilities, valuation methods, liquidity requirements, and restoration process before claiming full coverage. Published accounts distinguish currency, reserve balances, and private bank deposits. The same asset cannot be counted twice or pledged against incompatible obligations.

Asset coverage does not independently guarantee purchasing power or eliminate banking risk. Exceptional issuance follows the book's congressional emergency authorization, sunset, and published coverage-restoration schedule. Central-bank liquidity supports solvent institutions against sound collateral; Treasury recognizes subsidies and fiscal losses. Ordinary operating deficits receive no concealed monetary financing.

The existing Digital Dollar Standard preserves a plain one-for-one representation of the dollar, rejects programmable behavioral restrictions, and protects physical cash.

Revenue and the three ledgers

The existing tax structure remains the revenue basis: ten percent individual liquid-income tax, fifteen percent corporate tax, and the book's specified exemptions, contributions, and other revenue instruments. The family-home exemption and qualifying small-business land exemption remain intact. This framework does not import the concept document's progressive income surcharge or a land tax on primary residences.

The existing Three-Ledger Budget Standard separately presents General Government, Healthcare, and Social Security, followed by one reconciled consolidated account. Dedicated receipts serve their stated purposes. General Government transfers appear as spending on the sending ledger and funding on the receiving ledger, then disappear as internal transfers upon consolidation. One dollar becomes one dollar of national revenue.

The proposed accounting supplement publishes both the actual transition budget and the mature replacement-system scenario prominently. Transition costs, loan losses, guarantees, capital works, emergency deficits, interest, reserve contributions, and debt financing remain visible. Projected mature surpluses do not pay today's bills. Fines, speculative recoveries, asset appreciation, and unmeasured growth do not finance recurring commitments.

Permanent promises require independently scored recurring revenue or recurring expenditure reductions. Congress closes any remaining gap through explicit legislation within the book's tax framework and stated spending priorities before enlarging permanent commitments. A framework cannot promise every expenditure, every tax preference, rising public investment, and debt retirement without showing the arithmetic.

Debt reduction and Treasury financing

The proposed ten-year plan covers primary spending, capital works, reserves, interest, and contingent liabilities. Treasury publishes a rolling twelve-month maturity and cash map alongside it. Debt held by the public supplies the principal market-financing measure; gross debt, trust-fund obligations, and public financial assets remain separately visible. A domestic creditor remains a creditor.

Let d be the previous year's public-debt-to-GDP ratio, i the effective nominal portfolio interest rate, and g nominal GDP growth. Before stock-flow adjustments, the primary surplus needed to stabilize the ratio is:

Required primary surplus / GDP = [(i − g) / (1 + g)] × d.

Reducing that ratio by one percentage point annually requires another one percent of GDP in primary surplus, with stock-flow adjustments separately included. Falling debt relative to GDP does not necessarily reduce the dollar debt. Nominal debt retirement requires an overall surplus after interest.

After demonstrated recovery, the proposed initial adjustment improves the structural primary balance by half a percentage point of GDP annually until the independently scored requirement is met. The plan publishes the intervening debt peak and stress-tests higher interest rates, lower real growth, renewed fuel disruption, and guarantee losses. Congress sets a scored pace appropriate to labor and construction capacity. Renewed emergencies activate temporary borrowing with a published return path.

Treasury maintains predictable auctions, diversified maturities, and a prudent cash buffer. Borrowing to buy back a bond replaces debt; it does not retire debt on a net basis. Longer maturities receive a cost-and-risk assessment. Monetary intervention addresses demonstrated market dysfunction, assessed through trading depth, spreads, settlement, auctions, and funding conditions together. A higher yield alone is not dysfunction and creates no promise of permanently cheap government borrowing.

Public funds and protected obligations

The existing National Inheritance and Reserve Fund retains its capitalization schedule: two percent of qualifying revenue in years one through three, four percent in years four through six, six percent in years seven through nine, nine percent in years ten through twelve, and twelve percent thereafter, alongside its designated capital receipts. Every transfer appears in the financing plan. Borrowing to capitalize a fund creates an asset and a liability, not free wealth.

The NIRF retains its permitted productive investments, prohibited speculative assets, competitive-series requirements, institutional governance, audit triggers, and protected principal. Only authorized withdrawals count as spendable Treasury receipts. The existing limit is half the preceding five-year average inflation-adjusted investment return after the accumulation period, subject to principal, liquidity, return, and audit safeguards. Unrealized gains do not become cash merely because a valuation rises.

The Citizen Wealth Fund retains its one-percent annual-budget allocation and distinct debt, project, and decennial-dividend purposes. Social Security obligations, citizen accounts, the Strategic Resource Reserve, and NLA portfolios remain legally distinct. Fund ownership neither cancels Treasury liabilities nor permits the same return to fund several promises.

The existing Emergency Revenue Bridge allows its specified draw of up to fifteen percent of the year's qualifying surplus, within a twenty-four-month emergency window, with repayment at the NLA's fixed rate. It protects principal and personal accounts. Proposed implementing accounts separately identify eligible current-year surplus, liquid funds available, the original ledger, and enforceable repayment. An estimate of future earnings never becomes an emergency draw.

Productive investment and fuel affordability

Every major proposed appropriation receives a financial assessment and a capacity assessment covering labor, energy, materials, equipment, transport, and imports. OMB sequences agency work to complete repairs and remove bottlenecks before simultaneous projects compete for the same scarce inputs. CBO scores costs independently; GAO verifies delivery.

Contracts state additional output, baseline production, milestones, measurement, expiration, and clawbacks. Payments purchase verified progress or delivered public value. Available private financing, planned output, and land windfalls do not become public achievements. Credit programs publish both standard and fair-value costs, losses, administrative expense, beneficiary concentration, and the public benefit actually delivered.

The existing Diesel Price Correction pursues diesel below gasoline through supply, reserves, and excise treatment. The attached concept's permanent half-gasoline retail subsidy is not adopted. Any separately approved price credit requires recurring financing, a reserve, independently audited delivered-cost benchmarks, eligible domestic final use, and stress estimates for higher diesel costs, lower gasoline prices, and increased consumption. Exports, fictitious deliveries, duplicate claims, and subsidized resale receive nothing. Treasury reports unsubsidized delivered cost alongside the consumer price. An expanding public payment that hides an unchanged shortage does not establish productive progress.

Banking protection, recovery, and public review

Proposed bank safeguards protect payment continuity and deposits under enacted guarantees. Solvent lenders receive collateralized temporary liquidity. Insolvent institutions enter resolution, with shareholders and legally loss-bearing creditors absorbing losses. Necessary public capital purchases enforceable equity or warrants. Borrowers receive lawful restructuring supported by repayment capacity; existing mortgages and public lending promises remain enforceable. Public policy protects access to housing and productive credit without guaranteeing speculative asset values.

Recovery requires normalized essential deliveries, stable employment and labor income, receding financial stress, and contained broad inflation across two consecutive quarters. Temporary support winds down progressively, while reserve maintenance and permanent commitments remain funded. Renewed deterioration reactivates authorized crisis provisions.

The dashboard reports real median disposable income, work hours needed to buy essentials, reliable delivered energy, freight costs, occupied housing, food access, and health outcomes alongside output, inflation, fiscal exposure, and debt. Weekly crisis measures and regular public accounts expose failure before it compounds. The nation judges the system by affordable life, delivered capacity, protected obligations, and an honest balance sheet.

Source and decision notes

Existing-book reference: the published v98.20 policy book. Its tax framework, protected ledgers, public funds, NLA terms, resource-backed dollar, NMA replacement, cash protections, and existing fuel objective supply the inherited commitments.

Concept reference: American Fiscal Policy for Recovery and Prosperity, supplied as American_Fiscal_Policy_2030.docx. Its crisis diagnosis, supply repair, refinancing analysis, productive purchasing power, and debt arithmetic inform the proposed framework. Its hypothetical 2030 prices and rates are planning examples, not claims about current conditions. Its Economy Czar, progressive financing surcharge, primary-residence land-tax pilot, and automatic permanent half-gasoline subsidy are excluded.

Proposed decisions: crisis thresholds and deadlines, facility sunsets, recovery test, purchasing-power range, transition accounting, debt-adjustment pace, capacity assessments, and added banking safeguards remain unadopted drafts. No budget estimate in this framework establishes that the replacement system is fully financed; the independently scored transition and mature budgets must do that work before enactment.
